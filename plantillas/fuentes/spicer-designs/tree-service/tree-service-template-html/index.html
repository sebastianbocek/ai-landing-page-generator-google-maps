Judge it yourself first

What an arborist actually looks at.

Two of these six are genuinely urgent, three are worth a look, and one is not a problem at all. Publishing this costs us the jobs where nothing needed doing, which is rather the point.

Fungal brackets on the trunk

high

Brackets are the fruiting body of decay that is already well advanced inside the stem. By the time they appear, a significant proportion of structural wood is usually gone.

A crack or split at a fork

high

Included bark unions — where two stems grow tightly together with bark trapped between them — fail suddenly and often in still weather rather than storms.

Deadwood above something you value

medium

Deadwood in the crown is normal on mature trees. It only matters when there is a target underneath: a patio, a path, a parking space, a neighbour's conservatory.

Recent change in lean

high

A tree that has always leaned is usually fine. One that has moved recently, with soil cracked or heaved on the opposite side, is a different matter entirely.

Thinning crown, small leaves

medium

Usually stress rather than structural danger — drought, root damage from building work, or compaction. Worth assessing, rarely worth felling.

It is simply very large

low

Size is not risk. Most of the large trees we are called to assess are healthy, structurally sound and will outlive everybody reading this.

Before anything is cut

Your tree may be protected.

Felling or even heavily pruning a protected tree is a criminal offence carrying an unlimited fine, and the responsibility sits with the owner rather than the contractor. Plenty of people have no idea theirs is protected.

We check the register before quoting. A firm that does not is exposing you, not itself.

Even without a specific order, trees in a conservation area need six weeks notice before any work.

Questions

Including why we will not top a tree.

Is my tree dangerous?+

Size is not risk. The hazard signs on this page are what an arborist actually looks for — fungal brackets, included bark unions, deadwood over a target, a recent change in lean. Most large trees we are called to assess are healthy and stay exactly where they are.

Can I fell a tree in my own garden?+

Not if it carries a preservation order or stands in a conservation area, and plenty of owners have no idea theirs does. Felling a protected tree is a criminal offence carrying an unlimited fine. We check the register before quoting, every time.

Will you top my tree?+

No. Topping removes the crown indiscriminately, forces weak regrowth from the wounds, invites decay into every cut and shortens the tree's life. Crown reduction to a specification achieves what people actually want and the tree survives it.

Why is your quote higher than the man with a chainsaw?+

Rigging, ground protection, insurance and qualified climbers. A tree dropped rather than lowered is cheaper right up until it hits something, and at that point your insurer will ask who did the work and whether they were certified.

What happens to the wood?+

Chipped and taken away as standard, or cut to length and stacked if you want it. Say which at the quote — it makes no difference to the price.
