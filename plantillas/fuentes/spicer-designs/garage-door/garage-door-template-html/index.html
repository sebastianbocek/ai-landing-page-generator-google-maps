Genuinely dangerous

Two parts of this door can hurt you.

A garage door is the heaviest moving object in most homes, and the spring holding it is under enormous tension. This is not a warranty badge — it is the four things actually worth knowing.

The monthly auto-reverse test takes fifteen seconds and is the single most useful habit if there are children or pets in the house.

01

Never wind a torsion spring yourself

It holds enough stored energy to break an arm, and the winding bars are not optional equipment. This is the job in this trade that regularly sends people to hospital.

02

Test the auto-reverse monthly

Lay a roll of kitchen towel flat under the door and close it. If it does not stop and reverse on contact, stop using the door.

03

Check the photo-eyes are aligned

Both lights steady, four to six inches off the floor, facing each other. A blinking light means misaligned, not broken.

04

Never leave it part-open

A partly open door on a failing spring is the position it is most likely to come down from.

Openers

One question decides this.

Is there a bedroom above the garage? If yes, belt drive. If no, chain drive is cheaper and will outlive the house.

Belt drive

Quietest by a wide margin. Worth it when there is a bedroom over the garage, which is the only question that matters here.

Chain drive

Loudest and cheapest, and effectively indestructible. Fine in a detached garage.

Direct drive

Motor travels along the rail, one moving part. Quiet and expensive.

Battery backup

The one upgrade everybody wishes they had bought during a power cut. Roughly a hundred dollars.

Questions

Mostly about springs.

Can I replace a spring myself?+

We would rather you did not, and this is not sales talk. A torsion spring holds enough stored energy to break an arm, the winding bars are specialist tools, and improvising with screwdrivers is how people lose fingers. It is the one job in this trade that regularly hospitalises DIYers.

Why replace both springs?+

They wear at the same rate and have done the same number of cycles. Replacing one means the other fails within months, and you pay a second call-out to fix a job you already paid for.

Is my door worth repairing?+

Usually, and we will tell you when it is not. Rusted-through sections and a badly bent track are repairs that keep needing repairing. We would rather say so than take the money twice.

How long does a repair take?+

Springs and cables are typically ninety minutes and done on the first visit. Opener gear kits are similar. Anything needing a panel is ordered and fitted once it lands.

Do you fit doors you did not supply?+

Yes, and we service other firms' installations without any difference in price. There is no penalty for having bought elsewhere.
