A whole day

What happens at eleven in the morning.

This is the question every parent has and almost no nursery answers. So here is the entire day, in order, including the bits that are just free play.

07:30

Doors open, free play

Quiet start. Breakfast available until nine for anyone who needs it.

09:15

Welcome circle

Register, weather, what is happening today. Five minutes, not twenty.

09:30

Outdoors

Every room, every day, unless it is genuinely unsafe. Not cancelled for drizzle.

11:00

Focused activity

Small groups. Messy play, construction, early phonics in Meadow.

11:45

Lunch

Cooked on site, eaten together at tables, staff sitting with the children.

12:30

Sleep or quiet time

Those who sleep, sleep. Those who do not get books and a dim room rather than being made to lie down.

14:15

Outdoors again

Second session. This is when the garden is busiest and the best photographs happen.

15:30

Tea

A proper meal rather than a snack, so nobody goes home ravenous.

16:15

Free play, staggered pickup

Calm end. Your keyworker hands over in person and tells you about the day.

The first week

What everybody is actually anxious about.

Not the curriculum, not the garden. The first morning you walk away. Three settling sessions come before any full day, all free of charge, and you are present for the first.

If more are needed, more happen. Some children want six, which is normal, and nobody will make it feel like a problem or add it to your bill.

01

A visit with you, one hour

You stay the whole time. Nothing is expected of your child except being in the room.

02

A short session without you

Usually ninety minutes. You go and have a coffee somewhere, and we ring if it is not working.

03

A half day

Including a meal, which is often the thing that decides whether a child is comfortable somewhere.

04

Then the first real day

And if any of the above needs repeating, it gets repeated, at no charge. Some children need six sessions and that is entirely normal.

Questions

Asked at every visit.

What are your ratios, actually?+

One to three under eighteen months, one to four for toddlers, one to eight in preschool. The preschool figure is well below the legal minimum of one to thirteen, and it is where the difference is felt most. All three are printed at the top of this page rather than buried in a policy document.

What happens in the first week?+

Three settling sessions before the first full day, all free, with you present for the first. If more are needed, more happen — some children need six and nobody will make that feel like a problem.

What is not included in the fee?+

Nappies and formula. Everything else — all meals, snacks and milk, waterproofs, boots, sun cream, messy-play clothes and local outings — is in the fee rather than added later.

How will I know what my child did today?+

Your keyworker hands over in person at pickup and tells you properly. Under-twos also get a written log covering sleep, food and nappies. Photographs go out the same day rather than being saved for a newsletter.

Can I visit unannounced?+

Yes, during opening hours, and you never need a reason. A setting that requires notice before you can see it is telling you something.
