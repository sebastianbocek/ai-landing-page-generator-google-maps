Six steps before any colour

This is where a cheap quote is cheap.

None of it shows in a photograph, which is exactly why competitors lead with photographs. Every one of these steps is on the quote as a line rather than assumed.

01

Everything is moved or covered

Furniture centred and sheeted, floors run wall to wall, sockets and handles removed rather than cut around.

02

Wash down

Sugar soap on anything previously painted. Paint does not stick to grease, and kitchens and skirtings are always greasier than they look.

03

Fill, then fill again

First fill shrinks as it dries. Anything deeper than a hairline gets a second pass, which is the step most often skipped.

04

Sand to a flat surface

By hand at edges, machine on open walls, dust-extracted. You should not be able to feel a repair with your palm.

05

Prime bare and repaired areas

Every filled patch and every bare surface. Skipping this is why patches flash through a finish coat in raking light.

06

Caulk the joints last

Where wall meets woodwork, after priming and before finish coats, so the line stays sharp for years rather than months.

Products

Named on the quote.

“Trade quality paint” covers everything from contract emulsion at eight dollars a litre to a washable matt at forty. The product and the finish level are written down.

Trade emulsion

Named on the quote, with the finish level. Contract emulsion and a washable matt are not the same product at the same price.

Water-based woodwork

Non-yellowing on whites and quick to recoat. What we use unless you specifically want oil.

Oil-based woodwork

Harder wearing and slower. Available on request; it will yellow on a pure white over time and we will say so.

Exterior systems

Masonry and joinery products from one manufacturer so the warranty holds as a system.

Questions

Mostly about price.

Why is your quote higher?+

Usually because it includes preparation somebody else left out. Ask any cheaper quote how many coats, what happens to filled areas, and whether bare timber is primed — the answers normally explain the whole gap.

How many coats do I get?+

It is stated per surface on the quote rather than assumed. Most previously painted walls take two over primed repairs; bare timber takes four including primer and undercoat. The table on this page is what we work to.

Can you paint over damp or mould?+

No, and we will not. Painting over it hides it for one season and then it comes back through the finish, at which point you have paid twice. We will tell you what needs fixing first, even though it costs us the job that month.

How long will it take?+

A written schedule with a start and finish date. Exterior work is weather-dependent and we will be honest rather than optimistic about that — masonry below 8°C or onto damp is a wasted coat.

Do you move furniture?+

Yes, it is included. Rooms are cleared to the centre and sheeted, sockets and handles come off rather than being cut around, and everything goes back at the end.
