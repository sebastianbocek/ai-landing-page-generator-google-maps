pH

Ideal 7.4–7.6

The most important number on the list. Fix this before adding more chlorine.

Too low

Corrodes the heater, etches plaster, and strips the plaster of its finish.

Too high

Chlorine stops working. At pH 8.0 your chlorine is roughly a fifth as effective as at 7.4.

01

Test and dilute if needed

High cyanuric acid means shock will not work at any dose. Sometimes the first step is draining a third of the pool.

02

Balance pH first

Shocking at pH 8 wastes most of the chemical. This is why some recoveries take a week and others take three days.

03

Shock, then hold the level

One dose is not a treatment. Chlorine is held elevated for 48–72 hours with the pump running continuously.

04

Filter, clean, filter again

Questions

Two of these have counter-intuitive answers.

Why does my water keep going cloudy?+

Almost always calcium hardness or a filter running too few hours, rather than a chlorine shortage. Chlorine is what people reach for and rarely what is at fault, which is why every reading gets emailed to you after each visit.

Do I need weekly service?+

In season, yes for most pools. Out of season a fortnightly visit is usually enough, and we will move you onto it rather than keep billing weekly for work the pool does not need.

How much does a green pool cost to fix?+

It is published on this page rather than quoted after we hear how worried you are. Most recoveries take three to five days and the price depends on volume and severity, not on urgency.

Why can I smell chlorine so strongly?+

That smell is chloramines — chlorine that has already reacted and is spent. Strong chlorine smell means you need more free chlorine, not less, which is the exact opposite of what most people conclude.

Should I run the pump at night?+

Run it when the pool is being used and for enough total hours to turn the water over. Night running is cheaper on tariffs but does nothing for daytime chlorine loss, so most pools want a split.
