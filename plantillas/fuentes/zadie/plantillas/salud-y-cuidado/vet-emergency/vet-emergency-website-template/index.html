Know the Signs

When to Bring Your Pet In

If you're unsure whether it's an emergency, call us. We'd rather see your pet and send you home with peace of mind than have you wait too long.

Difficulty Breathing

Labored breathing, wheezing, choking, or blue/purple gums are life-threatening signs that require immediate attention.

Seizures

Any seizure lasting more than a few minutes or multiple seizures in a row warrants an emergency visit right away.

Trauma or Injury

Hit by a car, fall from height, bite wounds, or any significant physical trauma. Even if your pet seems okay, internal injuries can be hidden.

Toxin Ingestion

Chocolate, xylitol, grapes, medications, rat poison, antifreeze, or any suspected poisoning. Time is critical for decontamination.

Bloat / Distended Abdomen

A swollen, hard belly with retching or restlessness can indicate gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), a life-threatening condition.

Not Eating 24+ Hours

Refusing food for more than 24 hours, especially combined with lethargy or vomiting, can signal a serious underlying issue.
