Common Solar Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most from homeowners considering solar.

The average residential solar system costs between $15,000 and $25,000 before incentives. After the 30% federal tax credit, that drops to $10,500 to $17,500. The exact cost depends on your roof size, energy consumption, and panel selection. We provide a detailed, transparent quote with no hidden fees after your free consultation.

The physical installation typically takes 1-3 days for residential systems. The full timeline from signed contract to power-on is usually 6-8 weeks, which includes engineering design, permitting (2-4 weeks depending on your city), installation, inspection, and utility interconnection approval.

We use industry-standard flashing and sealant at every attachment point, and our installations carry a 10-year roof penetration warranty. Standard grid-tied solar systems will shut off during a power outage for safety reasons. This is required by law to protect utility workers. If you want backup power during outages, you'll need a battery storage system. With a battery, your home can operate independently from the grid, keeping essential circuits running until power is restored.

Over the course of a year, even in areas with variable weather, solar panels perform reliably. We account for local weather patterns in our system design and savings projections.
